# City Redesign: Existing Project Assessment and Technical Feasibility Review

Prepared by the model that built Explore mode (the 3D city), for the model that will write the technical specification and roadmap. Date: 14 November 2026.

**How to read this document**

- **Verified** means read from the code or measured in the running app on the date above. All of section 1 is verified unless a line says otherwise.
- **Decided** means settled by the owner's answers, recorded in section 8.1. The document was revised around them on the same day.
- **Recommendation** and **Estimate** mean my judgement. Budgets and effort sizes are proposals, not measurements.
- Nothing was changed in the project's code for this review.

## Summary

What exists:

1. **Engine.** The city is hand-written three.js (version 0.187, WebGL2), with no react-three-fiber and no game engine. Recommendation: keep it. Nothing in the request needs a different engine.
2. **Why it looks like glowing boxes.** The city is fully procedural: stretched cubes, planes and a few primitives, drawn by custom shaders, with text painted on 2D canvases. There are no 3D models, no texture files, no real lighting and no shadows. The neon outlines are what separates one unlit box from the next.
3. **It is very light.** About 78,000 triangles and 60 to 125 draw calls per frame, about 175 KB of compressed JavaScript, and nothing else to download except the projects' pictures. There is a lot of room to add detail.
4. **New projects already appear automatically**, because the city is rebuilt from the CMS each time it loads. But the layout is not stable: the size, side and position of existing buildings shift when a post is added.
5. **The engine has no input or event layer.** Look-around, a reactive robot and an in-city detail view all need pointer picking, an event bus and a small command interface. None of that exists yet.
6. **State of the code.** All of Explore mode is uncommitted on branch `feature/explore-mode`. It has never been run on a real phone, in Safari or on a low-end GPU.

What the owner decided:

7. **Every model is generated by code, in a stylised cyberpunk look.** No bought or downloaded assets, no realistic humans. Quality now depends on how well the generators are written and tuned; this is the largest and riskiest part of the work.
8. **The side streets come into use**, holding projects and details. The whole engine assumes one straight street today, so this is the main architectural change.
9. **At most 10 projects and logs**, with the graduation project and the co-op in fixed special places. A fixed city plan with spare places replaces open-ended growth.
10. **Phones are a first-class target**, from the iPhone 11 and Galaxy S20 up, with Low quality as the default. Loading may take up to a minute on a low device and 5 to 15 seconds on others.
11. **The robot uses no run-time AI.** Each building's texts are generated when the post is added and then saved. **Sound is wanted.**

Size and next step:

12. **Size: Major.** Section 8.2 lists eight points the answers leave open, each with a default the specification can assume.

### 1.4 How the parts communicate

```
Firestore (posts, settings/projects, skills)
   │  server, cached
   ▼
page.js ── buildTour({ posts, menu, skills, lang }) ──► tour (plain data)
   ▼
<ExploreMode lang dict tour>                      (client)
   ├─ device.js verdict ─► <DeviceGate> on phones, or a "not supported" notice
   ├─ import("…/engine/createExploreEngine")      (separate download)
   │     createExploreEngine({ host, tour, quality, calm, family, rtl, text,
   │                           imageFor, onReady, onStage, onFrame, onSlow })
   │     returns { dispose }
   └─ <ExploreHud stage …>                         (HTML over the canvas)
```

- **Engine to React**: four callbacks. `onStage({ kind, index })` fires when the visitor enters the intro, a cruise leg, a stop or the finale. `onFrame({ progress, guideX, guideY, guideVisible })` fires every frame; React uses it to move the speech bubble and the progress bar by writing styles directly, without re-rendering. `onReady` and `onSlow` fire once.
- **React to engine**: nothing after construction. The only live input is the page's scroll position, which the engine reads from `window.scrollY` every frame. There is no command API and no event bus.
- Changing `quality`, `tour`, `dict` or `lang` disposes the engine and builds a new one (it is a React effect dependency).
- The engine is one factory function with closure state. Its modules receive a shared context `{ scene, shared, palette, random, layout, settings }`. There is no entity or component system, no scene description format and no asset manager.

### 1.5 Tour and layout

`buildTour` (plan.js):

- One stop per published post. Order: ordinary projects by date, oldest first; then the graduation project; then the co-op training.
- `kind` is detected by regular expressions on the slug and titles (graduation: `/graduat|تخرج/`; co-op: slug `coop` or `/co-?op|تدريب تعاوني/`).
- `theme` (which hologram floats above the building) is detected by regular expressions on the slug and English title: car, document, robot, globe, nodes, chart, else a gem. The two landmarks get a graduation cap and a bank front.
- `tier = index / (count - 1)`, from 0 to 1. It sets how large and modern a building is.
- `side` alternates by index, starting on the right.
- `inProgress` comes from the projects menu (progress below 100). Menu items below 100 with no post become "upcoming" construction sites (at most 3).
- Street sign words are the skill names of at most 14 characters (at most 28).
- Each stop carries: id, slug, kind, theme, district, side, tier, title, description, date, year, image URL, GitHub and live URLs, progress, inProgress, and `hasDetails` (computed but used nowhere). The post's long `content` text and its tags are **not** sent to the client.

Route and scroll (plan.js):

- The route is one straight street along -z: 64 m to the first stop, 104 m between stops, 124 m after the last. A cross street lies 58% of the way along each leg.
- `tourPosition(progress, stops)` turns scroll progress (0 to 1) into a position counted in legs, easing each leg so the flight slows near every stop (`DWELL = 0.72`). `routeZ` turns that into metres.

`createLayout` (layout.js):

- Street cross-section: 7 m half road (four lanes), 5 m sidewalk, facades at 12.6 m from the centre line.
- Footprints: an ordinary project is `13 + 9·tier` wide, `11 + 6·tier` deep and `9 + 62·tier^1.35` high. The graduation hall is 36 × 18 m and 58 m high. The bank tower is 26 × 20 m and 104 m high.
- Each stop's building is turned to face the point where the camera pauses, set back until its nearest corner clears the sidewalk, and stepped toward the camera until it clears the next cross street. A square lies in front of it.
- `tierAt(z)` gives the 0-to-1 "grandness" of any point on the street, used by ordinary buildings, lamps, traffic and people.

### 1.6 Rendering, lighting and materials

- `WebGLRenderer` with antialiasing, ACES filmic tone mapping (exposure 1.2), sRGB output. Camera: 60° vertical field of view (72° when the screen is taller than wide), near 0.3 m, far 1,600 m.
- Post-processing (High and Medium): the scene is drawn into a half-float buffer (4× MSAA on High), then `UnrealBloomPass` (strength 0.34, radius 0.45, threshold 1.05), then `OutputPass`. The bloom's bright-pass shader was replaced to select by the brightest colour channel and to clamp values. Lite draws straight to the screen with no bloom.
- **There is no lighting for the city.** Every city surface is an unlit `ShaderMaterial` or `MeshBasicMaterial`. Shape comes from emissive colour, simple gradients by height and normal, and bloom. No shadows, no environment map, no reflections. The "wet road" is a view-angle sheen in the ground shader plus static additive streaks.
- Only the robot is lit: `MeshPhysicalMaterial` and `MeshStandardMaterial`, with one hemisphere light, one directional light and three point lights that travel with it.
- Fog is a custom exponential-squared haze (density 0.0052) whose colour depends on height, implemented in every custom shader through shared uniforms. Built-in materials use `scene.fog` with a single colour, so the two differ slightly.
- Nothing is frustum-culled (`frustumCulled = false` everywhere). Landmark groups are hidden beyond 440 m.
- All geometry is generated at load from a fixed random seed, so every visitor sees the same city.

### 1.8 Camera and navigation

- A rail camera. The page contains a tall empty element (`.explore-track`, `(stops + 1) × 140` viewport heights). Native document scrolling (wheel, touch, keyboard) is the only way to move. The page's scrollbar is hidden.
- Each frame: scroll progress → eased tour position → smoothed (exponential follow, 4.2 per second) → z. The camera's x is a small sine sway that blends to a per-stop offset. Its height is `4.3 + 3.9·tier`. `lookAt` blends between a point 40 m ahead and the stop's focus point (3 m under its name sign) by a 0-to-1 `focus` weight, and to the end plaza at the finish.
- The mouse moves the camera by up to 0.6 m sideways and 0.35 m vertically (parallax). It does not rotate the view.
- There is no drag, orbit, zoom or click handling in the 3D scene. The canvas has no pointer handlers and the engine has no `Raycaster`.
- Overlay navigation: one dot per stop and previous/next buttons, which animate the page scroll. The Exit button and Esc return to the main site.

### 1.9 Project cards

- HTML (React, Tailwind, framer-motion fade), fixed at the bottom on the side away from the building; a bottom sheet on phones. Shown while the stage is a stop.
- Content: stop number, month and year, title, an "under construction" chip, the description clamped to 2 to 4 lines, GitHub and live links, previous/next.
- "Open in portfolio" is a Next `<Link>` to `/{lang}#log-{slug}`. This is the redirect to the homepage that the owner wants removed. There is no detail view inside the city.

### 1.10 The AI bot

- **It is not AI inside Explore mode.** Both its motion and its words are scripted.
- Motion (`guide.js`): it holds a position ahead of the camera on the side of the next stop, turns to face forward while the visitor scrolls forward, and at a stop moves to the far side from the building, faces the visitor and raises a hand toward the building. It blinks, and its mouth moves for 2.6 s after each stage change.
- Words (`guideLine` in `ExploreHud.jsx`): one static dictionary string per stage, from 11 strings per language: intro, "Next stop: {title}", "one more stop", first stop, three rotating generic lines, unfinished project, graduation, co-op, finale.
- It does not react to scroll speed, direction, clicks or idle time.
- The speech bubble is HTML, positioned from the robot's projected screen position.

### 1.12 Limitations and technical debt

1. **No assets and no asset pipeline.** No loader, no texture compression, no streaming, no caching strategy.
2. **No lighting model.** Removing the glow outlines without adding one leaves flat dark boxes.
3. **Detail lives in a few large GLSL strings** inside `buildings.js` and `ground.js`. They are hard to extend and untested. Two shader bugs were hit during development: `pow` of a negative number produced invalid values that the bloom spread over the whole frame, and per-building values needed `flat` interpolation to stop window noise.
4. **Layout rules are formulas tied to the index.** Tier is relative to the number of stops, side is index parity, landmarks are forced last. Cross streets are periodic in the ground shader, so legs must be equal and the street straight.
5. **Meaning is inferred by regular expressions** on titles and slugs (graduation, co-op, hologram theme).
6. **No input layer.** No picking, no event bus, no command API, no camera modes. `frame()` is one long function and the robot's behaviour is one update function with implicit state.
7. **Movement is bound to the document's scroll height.** Nothing can pause, lock or redirect it.
8. **Quality is fixed when the engine is created.** No level of detail, no culling, no user setting.
9. **The tour payload lacks the long text and tags.**
10. **Tests cover pure logic only.** No visual regression test, no performance test, and no fixed-time hook for one (the city is deterministic by seed but animated by wall-clock time).
11. **Never profiled on phones or weak GPUs.** The automatic downgrade is untested in the field.
12. **The work is uncommitted.**
13. **3D text is fixed-resolution canvas textures.** It will blur if the camera is allowed closer.
14. **The robot's model is duplicated** from `CoreBot3D.jsx` instead of shared.

## 2. Feasibility assessment

Points settled by the owner's answers (section 8) are marked **Decided**.

### 2.1 Detailed buildings, and removing the glowing outlines

- **Today.** A building is one or a few stretched cubes. Windows, shop fronts and trim are painted by a shader. There is no geometry for windows, doors, ledges or corners.
- **Achievable?** Yes. It is the largest item and the one that changes the engine's architecture: the "add a box" interface must become a building assembly system.
- **Decided: every model is generated by code, in a stylised cyberpunk look.** The implementing model writes generators that build geometry and textures when the city loads. There are no model files, no bought or downloaded assets and no AI mesh tools.
- **What that decision means.**
  - The quality ceiling is "detailed and stylised", which is the look the owner chose. Photorealism is out of scope.
  - There are no licences to check and almost nothing to download. New projects stay automatic.
  - The cost moves from finding art to writing and tuning generators, and to the time the city takes to build itself on the visitor's device.
- **Removing outlines is trivial**: they come from three places (the trim term in the building shader, the roof rim, and the neon tube boxes that `landmarks.js` adds around bases, towers and screens, plus one strip under each rail bridge in `life.js`). Switching them off alone makes things worse, because nothing else defines the shapes. It must come with item 2.2.

What to build (recommendation):

1. **A facade generator.** A building is a footprint, a number of floors and a style. The generator emits real geometry: recessed windows with frames, floor bands and pilasters, cornices and parapets, setbacks, chamfered or rounded corners, entrances with canopies and steps, shop fronts, balconies with railings, awnings, fire stairs, pipes and cable runs, sign brackets, and roof equipment (tanks, air conditioners, ducts, antennas, dishes, billboards). three.js provides the tools: `ExtrudeGeometry` for profiles, `LatheGeometry` for round parts, `TubeGeometry` for pipes and neon tubes, and merged `BufferGeometry` with vertex colours.
2. **Style families along the route**: old town (masonry, small shops, warm light), midtown (mixed), the centre (glass and metal towers, holograms), the new district (sleek, with unfinished edges).
3. **A fixed set of generated variants per family** (for example 8 to 12), instanced many times with a per-instance tint, seed and signage. This bounds both build time and draw calls.
4. **Project and landmark buildings as registered generators** (section 4.3).
5. **Textures made at load** on canvases or in shaders: panel lines, wear, posters, shutters, and signs in Arabic and English. Bilingual neon signage would give this cyberpunk city its own identity.
6. **Depth without geometry**: interior mapping (fake rooms behind each window, about 40 lines of GLSL) and corner darkening baked into the vertices.
7. **Keep today's instanced box shader** for the far rows and for Low quality. It already looks right at a distance.

A practical requirement for model-written 3D: a development-only gallery page that shows each generator's output on a turntable at fixed angles. Shapes written in code can only be judged by looking at them, so the implementing model needs this page to review and regression-test its own work by screenshot.

- **What changes**: `createBlocks` and `layBuildings` in `buildings.js`; all of `landmarks.js`. New: the building generator, a material and lighting recipe, the gallery page. `layout.js` changes for section 4.
- **Libraries**: none needed. The glTF, Draco, Meshopt and KTX2 loaders are not needed under this decision.
- **Performance (estimate)**: 300,000 to 600,000 triangles on High if near buildings carry 2,000 to 8,000 each. Draw calls stay low because variants are instanced. Build time is the new cost: if generation takes more than about 2 s on the reference phones, move it to a Web Worker.
- **Risks**: the look depends entirely on how well the generators are designed and tuned, which makes this the main schedule risk. Repetition is the second risk; variants, signage and colour reduce it.
- **Difficulty**: Very large. Architectural change.

### 2.2 Lighting and materials (not in the request, required by 2.1)

- Hundreds of real dynamic lights are not possible in three.js's forward renderer. The standard recipe for a night city is: one moon direction and a sky term computed in the shaders for form; emissive windows and signs with bloom (exists); corner darkening baked into the geometry; light pools and streaks on the ground (exist); soft additive "wash" cards on facades; and optionally one small environment map rendered once from the sky dome so glass reflects something.
- Because all geometry is generated (2.1), the recipe belongs in the project's own shaders, sharing the existing fog. No standard-material path is needed.
- Real-time shadows: skip, or one directional shadow map on High only. Blob shadows under vehicles and characters are cheap and enough.
- Road reflections: screen-space reflections are too heavy. The `Reflector` add-on (a mirrored second render of the scene at half resolution) is possible for the road on High only, at roughly 60 to 80% more frame cost (estimate).
- **Difficulty**: Medium to large. **Risk**: tuning for a look that is vivid without being overdone. The owner has previously asked for saturated colours with faint glows.

### 2.3 Citizens

- **Today.** Box figures of about 210 triangles, limbs swung in the vertex shader, one draw call for all.
- **Decided: no realistic humans.** The look is stylised and all models are generated by code. Organic human bodies are also the hardest shape to generate in code.
- **Recommendation: robot citizens.** Three or four types (a biped, a hovering drone, a wheeled courier, a tall guard). Their parts are rigid, so today's instancing technique carries over with better shapes. They match the site's robot guide. They can also do things: wave, queue at a kiosk, sweep, carry a sign. If human figures are still wanted, hooded or helmeted cyberpunk silhouettes built from rigid parts are the workable form.
- **What changes**: `personGeometry` and the crowd shader in `life.js`; optionally behaviour (waiting at crossings, avoiding props).
- **Difficulty**: Medium. **Performance**: negligible at 300 to 800 triangles each.

### 2.4 Trees

- **Cause of the purple**: canopy colours are picked from pink, mint and violet outside the old town and drawn half emissive.
- **Options**: (a) natural trees in dark greens with warm uplights. Date palms would give the city a local identity and a recognisable silhouette. (b) Deliberately designed glowing trees in the accent colour, used sparingly on squares in the newest districts. (c) Planters, hedges, green walls and roof gardens.
- **Recommendation**: (a) as the default, (b) as an accent, so the "old to new" story also shows in the planting. All generated by code.
- **What changes**: `treeGeometry` in `ground.js` and the colour rule in `landmarks.js`. Add wind sway in the vertex shader.
- **Difficulty**: Small to medium. **Performance**: negligible; a few hundred instances at 300 to 600 triangles are affordable. Foliage cards need alpha-to-coverage on High for clean edges.

### 2.6 Sky

- **Today.** A dome shader with a gradient, horizon glow and faint noise clouds; star points; a canvas crescent; a skyline ring; one shooting star.
- **Neon clouds**: straightforward. Draw richer layered clouds with coloured rim light. To keep it free at run time, render them once into a texture at start and scroll that slowly.

### 2.10 Camera and navigation

- **Achievable?** Yes, on the current code. The camera maths is about 25 lines inside `frame()`.
- **Recommendation: stay on the rail, add two things.**
  1. *Look-around while moving.* Dragging turns the view within limits (about ±70° sideways, -10° to +40° up and down). It eases back to the guided direction when scrolling resumes. Scrolling still moves along the route.
  2. *Inspect at a stop.* Dragging orbits along a limited arc around the building (about ±55°), with a small zoom and two or three preset views (front, sign, roof). A "recentre" button is always shown.
- **Never**: free flight, WASD walking, roll.

- **Side streets** (section 4) mean the rail is no longer a straight line. The camera's sway, height and look direction must be computed in the route's local frame, not from z.
- **What changes**: extract a camera rig module with modes (rail, look, inspect, detail) and blended transitions; add a pointer input layer (new); add a recentre control to the overlay. The arcs can be validated against the layout's clear zones, so no collision system is needed.
- **Difficulty**: Medium. **Performance**: none.

### 2.11 A more interactive robot

- **Achievable?** Yes. No blocker, but it needs the missing input and event layer.
- **Decided: no run-time AI in the city.** Every building has its own texts. When a project is added, a small generator asks the owner's AI to write them and saves them for reuse.

Design (recommendation):

- **An event bus in the engine.** Sources: the scroll controller (speed, direction change, sustained fast scrolling, skipping a stop, idle time), stage changes, pointer picking, overlay actions (card opened, link clicked, language or quality changed), system events (quality downgrade, tab regained), the visitor's local time, and a small visit memory in `localStorage`.
- **Picking without a general raycaster**: test the pointer ray against the robot's bounding sphere and against the buildings' oriented boxes, which `layout.js` already knows.
- **A "dialogue director"**: a pure, unit-testable module like `plan.js`. A table of rules, each with a condition, a pool of lines, a priority, a cooldown and an optional animation. It picks the best eligible rule, avoids repeats, and never queues more than one line.
- **Two kinds of text.** Generic reactions (fast scrolling, clicks, idling) are authored once in the two dictionaries, with variants and placeholders such as `{title}`, `{year}`, `{progress}`. Building texts are saved with each post (below).
- **The robot's update function becomes a small state machine** with named animations: flinch, spin, dizzy, wave, nod, look at a target, celebrate, sleep.

The text generator (decided):

- When a post is created or edited in the admin dashboard, the dashboard calls a new admin endpoint. The endpoint asks the same Gemini setup the site already uses to write that building's lines in English and Arabic, and the dashboard saves them with the post. The city only reads saved text.
- Suggested fields, with two or three variants each: an arrival line, a "did you know" line, a line for the detail view, and a short tagline for the building's sign.
- Existing pieces to reuse: `src/lib/server/adminAi.js`, the `/api/admin/translate` and `/api/admin/prompt-assist` endpoints, the admin authentication, and the bilingual field components in `src/components/admin/translator/`. The dashboard already writes posts with the Firebase SDK.
- Also needed: the new fields in `normalizePost`, their inclusion in the tour, and a one-time run for the posts that already exist.
- Recommendation: show the generated text in the dashboard so it can be edited or regenerated. It is public text in two languages.

Example interactions:

| Trigger | Reaction |
|---|---|

### 2.12 Project card that expands in 3D

- **Today.** The card links to the homepage. The client does not have the post's long text.
- **Achievable?** Yes.
- **Recommendation: real HTML, animated as if it comes out of the 3D screen.**
  1. Clicking the card, the building or its screen puts the camera in a "detail" mode: it moves toward the building's screen.
  2. The engine reports the screen's four corners in screen space. The detail panel starts from that quad with a CSS `matrix3d` transform and animates to its final layout, while the 3D screen fades. To the visitor, the screen unfolds into the panel.
  3. The panel is ordinary HTML: sharp at any size, selectable, accessible, correct in Arabic, with working links. It can reuse the content of the main site's existing `ProjectDetails` (picture, date, title, tags, description, long text, links), without its "Ask AI" button.
  4. Closing reverses the animation.
- **Why not render the details inside WebGL**: long text as textures is blurry and inaccessible, and right-to-left layout would have to be rebuilt by hand. three.js's `CSS3DRenderer` can place real HTML in the 3D scene if the panel should tilt with the camera; that is a possible refinement, not a requirement.

- **Difficulty**: Medium. **Performance**: none.

- **Can the current engine carry the improvements?** Yes. ## 3. Engine and technology recommendation

**Keep three.js and improve the current implementation. Do not migrate.**

| Option | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Stay on three.js (hand-written) | **Recommended** | The limit is content and lighting, not the renderer. Generated geometry, custom shaders and instancing are exactly what the current code already does |
| Move the engine to react-three-fiber | Not recommended now | It would rewrite about 4,000 lines of engine code for no visible gain. The hot paths stay imperative anyway. It would help with events and HTML-in-3D, which the input layer in section 6 covers at lower cost |
| Babylon.js or PlayCanvas | Not recommended | Comparable capability, so a rewrite buys nothing. PlayCanvas would move scene authoring out of the repository |
| Unity or Unreal for the web | Not recommended | Multi-megabyte downloads, slow start, weak phone support, and they break the HTML overlay, the two languages, static generation and the on-demand loading that the site relies on. They also assume authored assets, which the owner has ruled out |
| three.js WebGPU renderer | Not now | It ships in the installed version but would require rewriting every custom shader in a new shading language. No requested feature needs it, and the floor phones must run it |

Technologies to add (recommendation):

- **Nothing new is required.** Geometry generation, Web Audio, Web Workers and CSS 3D transforms are all built into three.js or the browser.
- Optional: the `postprocessing` package (pmndrs), which merges bloom, grading, vignette and anti-aliasing into fewer passes than three's own chain. Worth it once more effects are added, and it matters most on phones.
- Optional: `troika-three-text` if sharp 3D text is needed at close range.
- Development only: a statistics overlay and a tuning panel.
- Not needed: model loaders and texture compression (no imported assets), a physics engine, a general raycasting accelerator, a camera-controls library (the constrained rig is simpler to write than to configure).
- Available later without changing engine: three.js 0.187 includes glTF, Draco, Meshopt and KTX2 loaders (checked), should authored models ever be wanted.

**Can the existing project reach the described quality without starting over?** Yes, for a detailed stylised cyberpunk city. That is the look the owner chose.

What carries over unchanged or nearly: the Next.js integration and on-demand loading, the content flow, the overlay, the quality governor, decals and glows, the sky, the seeded generation approach, the tests' pattern. What is generalised: the scroll and route maths, the layout solver, the ground, traffic and crowd (all currently assume one straight street). What is replaced or heavily extended: building construction, landmarks, materials and lighting, the robot's dialogue, the camera, and input. Estimate: about half of the engine's code is rewritten or substantially extended. Nothing forces a restart.

## 4. Scalability and architecture

### 4.1 What happens today when a project is added

It works with no code change: the post appears as a new stop the next time the page revalidates. But:

1. **Every building changes.** Tier is `index / (count - 1)`, so one more post changes the size and style of all others.
2. **Sides flip.** Side is index parity, and the two landmarks are forced to the end, so a new project moves both landmarks one leg further and to the other side.
3. **The street and the scroll get longer** by 104 m and 1.4 screens per post.
4. **Building types are code.** There are three builders. A new kind of building, or a new special post, means editing `landmarks.js` and the regular expressions in `plan.js`.
5. **The street must stay straight and evenly spaced**, because the ground shader computes cross streets from the leg length.
6. **Side streets are scenery.** Six exist today (one between every two stops). Each is a strip of road with buildings beside it; nothing stands on them and the camera cannot enter.

### 4.2 What the owner decided

- **Scale**: no more than 10 projects and logs in total. There are 7 today.
- **Side streets**: the "left and right routes" are the existing side streets. Projects and details will be placed on them, and navigation will change a little to reach them.
- **Landmarks**: the graduation project and the co-op get fixed special places. At the end of the street they look as if they were on the city's outskirts.

A cap of 10 changes the problem. The city does not need to grow without limit; it needs **a fixed plan with a few spare places, where every post keeps its place**.

### 4.3 Recommended direction

1. **A fixed city plan.** One street network (the main street and its side streets) with numbered places for buildings: about 14, for the cap of 10 plus spares. An empty place shows as a construction site or a small park, as the end of the street does today.
2. **Every post keeps its place.** A place is assigned once (stored with the post, or chosen in the admin dashboard) and never moves. A building's look is seeded by its slug, and its size comes from its own field, not from how many posts exist.
3. **The landmarks at the heart of the city.** Recommendation: the central junction becomes the centre. Its two side streets become short avenues, one ending in the graduation square and one in the bank tower's plaza, facing each other across the main street. The skyline is tallest there, and the towers are seen from far down the street. This also matches the dates: both fall in the middle of the timeline. The far end stays what it looks like: the frontier, where new projects are built.
4. **Grandness peaks at the centre.** Today the city grows grander all the way to the end. With central landmarks it should rise to the centre and then change character toward the new district.
5. **Side streets hold smaller buildings and details.** Each ends in a small court with one to three places. Which posts go there is a content choice (point 4 in section 8.2).
6. **One continuous tour (recommendation).** The route becomes a path of straight pieces and turns. It follows the main street; at a junction whose side street holds something, it turns in, circles the court and returns to the junction, then continues. Scrolling still moves along the path, so native scrolling, the dots and previous/next keep working, and nothing can be missed. A side street with nothing on it is not visited: the camera only glances down it. When a post is later given a place there, the route includes that street by itself.
   - Alternative: optional detours that the visitor chooses at each junction. This needs an on-screen choice and a scroll mechanism that is no longer the page's height, and visitors can miss content. It is a larger change than the owner described.
7. **Put the meaning in the content, not in code.** Add optional fields to a post, edited in the admin dashboard: building type, size, place, accent colour, and the saved robot texts (2.11). Everything has a default, so a post with none of them still appears.
8. **A registry of building types.** `registerBuildingType(key, generator)`. A new kind of building is one module. A new project is data only.
9. **The server builds one plan object**: a pure, tested function like `buildTour`. It outputs the street network, the places, which post is where, and the route. The engine consumes only that.
10. **Split the engine into systems**: environment (sky, fog, light), streets, ordinary buildings, places, life, guide, camera, interaction, sound.

### 4.4 What must change in the code

Almost everything in the engine is keyed to one coordinate, z. Using side streets means replacing that assumption:

| Today | Needed |
|---|---|
| `routeZ(position)` returns a z | A route function that returns a point and a direction along a path, plus distance along it |
| Camera sway, height and look target computed from z | Computed in the route's local frame |
| `tierAt(z)` | Grandness by district or by distance from the centre |
| Cross streets computed periodically in the ground shader | Roads passed in as data (junctions, side streets, courts), or road strips generated from the network |
| `layBuildings` fills rows along z | A function that lines both sides of any street segment |
| Lamps, walkers and cars loop along z | Lanes and sidewalks derived from street segments |
| Landmark visibility by distance in z | By distance along the route or in space |
| Stops alternate sides by index | Places come from the plan |
| `crossingDistance(z)` and the clear zones | Clearances computed from the network |

`plan.test.js` and `layout.test.js` show the pattern for testing the new plan: no building on a road or sidewalk, every place reachable by the route, every post on exactly one place.

### 4.5 No longer needed, because of the cap

- Loading the city district by district.
- Grouping several projects into one stop, or a separate "highlights" tour.
- Streets that grow by themselves.
- A virtual scroll controller, if the tour stays one continuous path.

Hide scrolling behind one small interface all the same (`getProgress`, `lock`, `flyTo`), because the detail panel and inspect mode need to lock it.

## 7. What I would do differently

Where the owner has since decided, the advice is marked.

1. **Do not aim for realism.** *Agreed by the owner: stylised cyberpunk, generated by code.* The one consequence to accept is that quality then depends on generator design and patient tuning, not on a library of assets.
2. **Do not delete the glow; give it a source.** Neon is this site's identity and the heart of a cyberpunk look, and the owner has asked before for vivid colours with faint glows. Replace outline tubes with light that comes from somewhere: signs, windows, strips set into the architecture, lamps.
3. **Keep the camera on its rail.** Add look and inspect. Free movement makes a guided portfolio tour worse.
4. **Script the robot.** *Agreed by the owner: no run-time AI; building texts are generated once and saved.* I would add a review step in the dashboard before generated text goes live.
5. **Show project details as HTML** that appears to unfold from the 3D screen, not as text inside WebGL.
6. **Fix the content model before the visuals.** Otherwise each new project reshuffles the redesigned city.
7. **Make the side streets part of one tour, not a choice.** *The owner wants them used.* They are still the most expensive single decision, because the whole engine assumes one straight street. A continuous route that turns into them keeps scrolling simple and guarantees nothing is missed.
8. **Prove one block on the real floor phones first**, before building the whole city. Phones are now the main target and nothing has ever run on one.
9. **Keep the automatic downgrade** even with Low as the default, and offer High clearly on devices that can take it. Otherwise most visitors never see the best version.
10. **Remove the phone warning screen.** It tells first-class visitors they are second-class.
11. **Sound.** *Agreed by the owner.* I would generate it in code first and start it from a "Start" control, so the browser allows it.
12. **Keep Explore mode optional and loaded on demand.** The classic site remains the fast, accessible, searchable version.

## Addendum: the rendering rebuild of 16 November 2026

Sections 1.6, 1.7, 1.11 and the constants above describe the city before this pass. This addendum records what replaced them. The engine is still hand-written three.js with every shape and texture generated by code.

### What the owner asked for

Graphics that no longer read as "a 2001 game with glow on it"; detail on buildings, streets, cars, robots and street sides; smoother, shorter scroll steps; small details such as steam; a real background in place of the flat skyline picture; roundabouts that are not empty; and the glitches on two project screens and some walls fixed.

### How the city is lit now

Nothing casts real light. Every material reads the same three sources (`LIGHTING` in `engine/shaders.js`):

| Source | File | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Light field | `engine/lightfield.js` | One half-float texture of the city seen from above, drawn once at load. Its colour is the light that lamps, signs, shop fronts, screens and doorways throw near the ground; its fourth channel marks building footprints. Walls, paving, cars, robots, trees and steam all sample it, so a pink sign tints whatever stands beside it. Surfaces take more of it when they face the brighter side of the map |
| Sky picture | `createExploreEngine.js` | A small cube map of the sky and the far city, taken once before the city is built. Glass, paint, water and metal mirror it, blurred by roughness |
| Mirrored road | `engine/mirror.js` | High quality only. Each frame the city is drawn a second time at half size from the camera's reflection below the ground; the asphalt looks that picture up, sharp in puddles and smeared toward the eye elsewhere. Low quality uses a cheaper sheen from the light field |

Shop sign colours are computed the same way in the shader and in JavaScript (`shopsOf` in `engine/kit.js`), so each open shop throws light of its own sign's colour.

### What else changed, by file

| File | Change |
|---|---|
| `engine/kit.js` | The material was rewritten: brick, concrete panels, render, cladding and dressed stone with relief; windows set back into the wall with frames, bars, blinds and curtains; shop fronts with real doors, shelves inside and names from a texture (`SHOP_NAMES`, Arabic and English); curtain walls with mullions, office floors, light strips and media walls; paving, worn road paint, water, ironwork, wood, traffic signals, lit panels and glazed entrances. Every repeating pattern is averaged over the pixel, which removed the shimmer on distant walls. New shapes: cone, ball, and `ringShape` for the paved rings of roundabouts |
| `engine/ground.js` | Wet asphalt with stones, patches, cracks and puddles; lamps and bollards redrawn |
| `engine/models.js`, `engine/life.js` | Cars are lofted bodies with wheels, glass, lamps, door seams and soft shadows, in seven kinds (sedan, taxi, hatchback, SUV, van, sports car, bus). Robots have jointed legs and arms that bend as they walk. Flying vehicles and trains were redrawn |
| `engine/nature.js` | Date palms with a smooth trunk and arching fronds; broad-leaved trees with lobed canopies |
| `engine/furniture.js`, `engine/streets.js` | Benches, bins, hydrants, bus shelters, vending machines, cafe tables, planters, traffic lights, manholes, drains, tree grates, dumpsters, crates, strings of lamps. Each side street now ends in a real roundabout: a road ring with lane paint, a planted island with a fountain, and a paved ring with lamps and benches (`roundaboutOf` in `city.js`) |
| `engine/effects.js` | Steam from manholes and alleys, exhaust vapour behind cars, fountain jets, dust in the light. All are placed by the graphics card from the clock alone |
| `engine/sky.js` | The flat skyline ring was replaced by a far city of real towers standing 215 to 780 m from the main street (about 3,000 instanced blocks: stepped towers, masts and warning lamps), with lit windows and strips of light |
| `engine/places.js` | The two reported glitches were surfaces lying in the same plane (the workshop's lit doorway and its roller door; the academy's screen and its tower wall). The workshop now has a real open garage; every other stop has glazed doors instead of a glowing rectangle; main-street stops have a paved forecourt |
| `plan.js`, `rig.js`, `ExploreMode.jsx`, `globals.css` | Scrolling. The page is now as long as the route (`createTour`, 14 pixels a metre), so one notch of a mouse wheel drives about 7 m while cruising and about 2 m near a stop; it was 15 to 40 m. The camera follows the scroll position as a critically damped spring |
